Why these numbers: Your TDEE is ~2,856 cal/day. A 400-calorie deficit puts you in fat-loss territory without tanking performance or muscle. At 20–22% body fat you have plenty of stored energy — the goal is to use that fat as fuel while keeping protein high enough to preserve (and build) muscle simultaneously. Protein at 2.2g per kg lean body mass is the gold standard for body recomp.

Whey protein 1 hour pre-workout increases muscle protein synthesis during the session itself — not just after. Don't train fasted if you're doing heavy compound movements.

Post-workout window: Your muscles are insulin-sensitive for 60–90 minutes post-training — this is when they absorb glucose most efficiently for glycogen resynthesis. White rice is ideal here (high GI, fast absorption). The leucine-rich chicken triggers mTOR signalling for muscle protein synthesis. Don't eat a fat-heavy meal post-workout — fats slow gastric emptying and blunt the insulin response you actually want right now.

With a recent back injury, getting 2–3 serves of fatty fish per week is genuinely therapeutic. It's not just nutrition, it's targeted anti-inflammatory medicine.

Overnight anabolism: You fast for 7–9 hours while sleeping. Casein is a micellar protein that forms a gel in your stomach — it releases amino acids slowly over 6–8 hours, keeping you in a net positive nitrogen balance all night. This is how serious lifters don't catabolise muscle overnight. If you don't have casein, 200g of Greek yoghurt or cottage cheese does the same job.

Add electrolytes on training days: A small pinch of Himalayan salt in your water bottle or a sugar-free electrolyte tab covers sodium, potassium and chloride losses from sweat. Especially important in hot Australian gyms. Dehydration of just 2% body weight reduces strength output by up to 10%.

Alcohol — the honest chat

Ethanol directly inhibits muscle protein synthesis by suppressing mTOR signalling — the same pathway creatine and protein are trying to activate. It also elevates cortisol, suppresses testosterone, disrupts sleep architecture (REM specifically), and blunts growth hormone release during sleep. A big night out can set your recovery back 48–72 hours.

Practical rule: 1–2 standard drinks max on social occasions. If you're going to drink, eat your full protein for the day first, drink plenty of water, and don't train the day after a big session — your nervous system and liver are already working overtime. This is body recomp, not boot camp — enjoy your life, just be smart about it.
